# AutoCheck QC - Complete Business, Product, Technical, Automation, Launch, SEO, Pricing, and Execution Roadmap

Prepared: September 8, 2026

## 1. Executive Summary

AutoCheck QC is an AI-assisted used-car pre-screening and inspection-booking platform for Montreal and Quebec. It helps buyers evaluate a used-car listing before they spend time visiting the vehicle, negotiating with the seller, ordering a full inspection, or making a deposit.

The core offer is simple:

> Paste any used-car ad before you buy. Get a clear AI risk report in 2 minutes. If the car looks worth it, book a mobile inspection.

AutoCheck QC does not replace a professional mechanical inspection. It sits one step earlier in the buying journey. It helps a buyer decide whether a car deserves more attention at all.

The product is built for people shopping on Facebook Marketplace, Kijiji, AutoTrader, private listings, and dealer inventory pages. The first market is Montreal and nearby Quebec regions: Laval, Longueuil, Brossard, South Shore, North Shore, and surrounding cities.

The business model starts with low operational complexity:

- Free Quick Check to capture leads.
- Paid Full AI Buyer Report at about $19.99 CAD.
- Premium Human Review at about $49.99 CAD.
- Inspection booking referrals with partner inspectors, targeting $30-$60 commission or service fee per completed booking.

The first revenue target is modest and practical: at least $500/month net after tools, hosting, AI, and payment costs. This can be reached with roughly 25 paid reports per month, or a smaller number of paid reports combined with inspection booking commissions.

The recommended V1 is not a large marketplace, scraping engine, or full mechanical-inspection company. V1 should be a focused web app with:

- landing page
- listing intake form
- optional screenshot/photo upload
- AI report generation
- Stripe payment
- email delivery
- admin dashboard or admin spreadsheet
- inspection booking form
- manual partner dispatch
- analytics and feedback tracking

## 2. Business Concept

AutoCheck QC protects used-car buyers before they commit time or money.

Most used-car inspection companies start with: "Book an inspection." AutoCheck QC starts earlier: "Check whether this car is worth inspecting first."

That difference matters because a used-car buyer may review 10-30 listings before choosing one car to inspect. Most buyers will not pay $170-$250 for a professional inspection on every possible car. But many will consider a lower-cost pre-screen if it helps them avoid obvious bad listings, ask better questions, negotiate better, or decide which car deserves a real inspection.

Core positioning:

- Brand name: AutoCheck QC
- Primary tagline: Don't buy a used car blind.
- Secondary tagline: Check the red flags before you pay, travel, negotiate, or inspect.
- Market: Montreal and Quebec used-car buyers.
- Language: bilingual French/English from the beginning.
- Main channels: SEO, Google Ads, useful local content, inspection partner referrals, and shareable reports.

The platform should be positioned around:

- buyer protection
- time savings
- negotiation help
- avoiding bad used-car purchases
- deciding when a professional inspection is worth it

It should not be positioned as "AI for fun" or as a guaranteed diagnostic tool.

## 4. Main Problem

Used-car buyers face a messy information problem. Listings are incomplete, seller claims are hard to verify, photos hide important defects, and buyers often feel pressure to act quickly.

Common buyer risks in Montreal and Quebec:

- hidden rust, especially underbody rust from winter road salt
- rebuilt or accident history not clearly disclosed
- unclear VIN or refusal to provide VIN
- seller does not allow inspection
- "selling for a friend" or mismatched ownership story
- suspiciously low price
- mileage that does not match condition
- no maintenance records
- check-engine light or warning lights hidden in photos
- transmission problems on models known for transmission risk
- hybrid battery concerns on older hybrids
- flood, taxi, rental, rideshare, or commercial-use history
- Quebec registration, lien, or transfer questions
- buyer does not know which questions to ask

The buyer's real decision is not only "is this car good?" The decision is:

- Should I message the seller?
- Should I ask for VIN, Carfax, maintenance records, and inspection permission?
- Should I spend time going to see it?
- Should I negotiate now or after inspection?
- Should I pay for a professional inspection?
- Should I avoid it completely?

AutoCheck QC turns unclear listings into a structured decision.

## 6. Full User Journey

1. The user searches online for help.

The user searches Google or asks in a local group for used-car inspection advice in Montreal or Quebec. They may search in English or French.

2. The user lands on AutoCheck QC.

The landing page immediately explains the promise: paste the car ad and get a clear risk report before visiting, negotiating, or paying for an inspection.

3. The user chooses a path.

Primary CTAs:

- Check this car now
- Book mobile inspection
- See example report

4. The user submits the listing.

The form allows:

- paste listing text
- paste URL if available
- upload screenshots/photos
- enter year, make, model, trim
- enter mileage
- enter asking price
- enter city
- select seller type: private seller, dealer, unknown
- enter VIN if available
- enter notes or concerns

5. The system extracts vehicle data.

The system extracts:

- make
- model
- year
- trim if available
- mileage
- price
- city/region
- seller type
- VIN if available
- accident history if mentioned
- rebuilt/salvage status if mentioned
- Carfax status if mentioned
- maintenance history if mentioned
- inspection availability if mentioned
- seller claims and promises
- visible photo concerns if images are submitted

6. The system identifies missing information.

The system flags missing items:

- VIN missing
- no maintenance records
- no interior photos
- no underbody photos
- no dashboard photo with engine running
- no tire/brake info
- no accident/Carfax history
- no ownership history
- no inspection permission
- no reason for sale

7. The user sees a Free Quick Check option.

The Free Quick Check returns:

- simple risk level
- 3 visible red flags
- 3 seller questions
- basic recommendation: Avoid, Ask more questions, Worth seeing, or Inspect

The free version requires email capture before delivery or after a limited preview.

8. The user chooses the paid report if they want more detail.

The paid report is framed as a low-cost pre-screen before a larger inspection cost or a costly buying mistake.

9. Payment is processed.

V1 should use Stripe Checkout or Payment Links to reduce build complexity. After payment, the user is redirected to a confirmation/report page.

10. AI generates the report.

The system produces a structured report with:

- Vehicle Summary
- First Impression
- Risk Score
- Price Check
- Biggest Red Flags
- Missing Information
- Common Problems for This Model
- Questions to Ask the Seller
- What to Check in Person
- Negotiation Points
- Inspection Recommendation
- Final Recommendation
- Next Step

11. The user receives the report.

The report is shown on-screen and sent by email. The admin also receives a copy.

12. The report recommends a next step.

The final recommendation must be one of:

- Avoid
- Ask more questions
- Worth seeing
- Worth professional inspection
- Strong candidate

13. The user can book a mobile inspection.

The inspection CTA appears when the car is not an obvious avoid. The booking form collects:

- buyer name
- phone
- email
- seller contact
- vehicle address
- preferred time windows
- vehicle details
- VIN if available
- urgency
- notes
- report ID

14. Partner mechanic or inspector receives the request.

In V1, dispatch can be manual:

- admin receives request
- admin contacts partner inspector
- partner confirms availability and price
- customer receives confirmation
- partner performs inspection
- AutoCheck QC receives referral/service fee

15. Follow-up is sent.

After the report and/or inspection, the system asks:

- Was the report useful?
- Did you inspect the car?
- Did you buy the car?
- Did you negotiate?
- Was the recommendation accurate?
- What happened after contacting the seller?

16. Feedback improves future reports.

Feedback is stored and reviewed to improve prompts, model-specific checklists, pricing logic, report wording, and partner quality.

## 14. AI Prompt and Report Logic

The AI system should use a two-step workflow:

1. Extract structured data from the listing and uploaded materials.
2. Generate a buyer-friendly report using the extracted data, safety rules, and report template.

### Step 1 - Structured Extraction

Extract:

- year
- make
- model
- trim
- mileage
- price
- city
- seller type
- VIN if shown
- accident history if mentioned
- rebuilt/salvage status if mentioned
- Carfax status if mentioned
- maintenance history if mentioned
- inspection availability
- seller claims
- visible photo concerns
- missing information

Extraction rule:

- If the information is not present, mark it as missing.
- Do not infer VIN results.
- Do not invent accident history.
- Do not invent Carfax details.
- Do not invent mechanical condition.
- Do not invent exact market value.
- Do not judge seller honesty as fact.

### Step 2 - Risk Analysis

The risk analysis should consider:

- age of vehicle
- mileage relative to year
- price compared with expected market range if available
- vague or short listing
- missing VIN
- seller refuses inspection
- rebuilt/salvage mention
- accident mention
- no maintenance records
- high-risk model/year patterns
- visible rust or damage in photos
- mismatch between price, mileage, condition, and seller claims
- urgency or pressure language
- dealer/private seller distinction

Risk levels:

- Low: listing looks complete and no obvious red flags are present, but inspection still recommended before purchase.
- Medium: normal used-car uncertainty; buyer should ask questions and verify details.
- High: several warning signs or major missing information.
- Very High: serious red flags such as inspection refusal, suspicious ownership story, rebuilt/accident concerns without documentation, or major visible damage.
- Unknown: not enough information to judge.

### Report Structure

Every paid report should follow this structure:

1. Vehicle Summary
2. First Impression
3. Risk Score
4. Price Check
5. Biggest Red Flags
6. Missing Information
7. Common Problems for This Model
8. Questions to Ask the Seller
9. What to Check in Person
10. Negotiation Points
11. Inspection Recommendation
12. Final Recommendation
13. Next Step

Final recommendation must be one of:

- Avoid
- Ask more questions
- Worth seeing
- Worth professional inspection
- Strong candidate

Required disclaimer:

> AutoCheck QC is not a replacement for a professional mechanical inspection. This report is an AI-assisted pre-screen based on the information provided. It helps you decide what to verify before buying.

### Prompt Rules

The AI should:

- extract structured vehicle data
- identify missing data
- analyze listing quality
- identify risk factors
- generate a common issue checklist
- generate seller questions
- generate negotiation points
- produce a clear final recommendation
- use plain language
- avoid overclaiming
- include the disclaimer
- distinguish between "visible risk" and confirmed defect
- recommend professional inspection when appropriate

The AI should not fabricate:

- accident history
- VIN results
- Carfax details
- RDPRM status
- mechanical condition
- exact market value
- seller honesty
- official SAAQ status
- legal conclusions
- insurance advice

Safe wording:

- "visible risk"
- "based on the listing information"
- "recommended to verify"
- "ask the seller"
- "confirm with a professional inspection"
- "possible red flag"
- "not enough information"
- "the listing does not show"
- "this should be checked before purchase"

### Quebec Buyer Context

The app can remind buyers to verify:

- vehicle ownership and registration steps through SAAQ or SAAQclic
- whether a vehicle is free of debt through RDPRM
- used-car dealer obligations and labels through the Office de la protection du consommateur
- VIN and vehicle history documentation
- actual odometer reading
- whether inspection is allowed before purchase
- insurance and registration requirements

These reminders must be educational and link to official sources. AutoCheck QC should not present itself as SAAQ, OPC, RDPRM, a lawyer, an insurer, or an official vehicle-history provider.

### Privacy Requirements

Users may submit:

- name
- phone number
- email
- seller information
- vehicle address
- VIN
- screenshots/photos
- listing text
- payment metadata
- feedback

Privacy principles:

- collect only what is needed
- store only what is useful
- separate payment handling through Stripe
- do not store full payment card details
- protect uploaded screenshots/photos
- restrict admin access
- allow deletion requests
- provide clear contact email for privacy requests
- avoid exposing seller contact information publicly
- define retention periods
- disclose use of AI processing
- disclose when data may be shared with inspection partners for booking

Suggested retention:

- free-check leads: 12 months unless user requests deletion
- paid reports: 24 months unless deletion is requested and legal/accounting obligations allow
- uploaded images: delete after 30-90 days unless needed for customer support
- inspection request details: 24 months for operational records

### Language and Quebec Considerations

Because the service is Quebec-focused, the public website and customer flows should be available in French and English. French should not be treated as an afterthought. Before scaling paid acquisition, review Quebec language, consumer, privacy, and advertising obligations with qualified counsel.

## Competitor Angle

Existing inspection companies usually sell the full inspection first.

Their message:

> Book a mechanical inspection.

AutoCheck QC's message:

> Check whether the car is worth inspecting first.

This creates a lower-friction entry point. A buyer who is comparing many cars may not book a full inspection immediately, but they may pay for a fast pre-screen. If the report shows the car is promising, AutoCheck QC can then route the buyer to a professional inspection.
